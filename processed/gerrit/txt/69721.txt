LU-17180 osc: run writeback work on an unbound workqueue

Patch "LU-17180 ptlrpc: don't block ptlrpcd too long" moved the
osc writeback and LRU work out of ptlrpcd threads, to
schedule_work(). schedule_work() queues to system_wq, which is
per-CPU: the work runs on the CPU that queued it. Most of it is
queued by the writing process itself, from osc_extent_release(),
osc_lru_reserve() and osc_lru_del(), so building RPCs and shrinking
the LRU now take CPU time from the writer, which is the bottleneck
of a single-stream buffered write. ptlrpcd threads are not bound
to one CPU, so before that patch this work ran beside the writer.

With dd pinned to CPU 3, writing 8 GiB to a 16-stripe file:
  brw_queue_work: 210ms of its 261ms ran on CPU 3
  lru_queue_work: 299ms of its 299ms ran on CPU 3
A drop from 1363 MiB/s to 1198 MiB/s was seen on real hardware.

Queue this work on a dedicated unbound workqueue instead, spanning
the CPUs of the CPT table as ptlrpcd threads do, so that the
scheduler runs it on an idle CPU. This work is needed to free
memory, as osc_lru_reserve() waits for it, so give the workqueue
a rescuer thread with WQ_MEM_RECLAIM, which system_wq lacks.
