LU-20403 lnet: keep reachable peer on local net delete

Deleting a local NI with "lnetctl net del" calls
lnet_peer_tables_cleanup() from lnet_shutdown_lndnet(), which in
turn calls lnet_peer_table_cleanup_locked(). When the net being
removed hosted a peer's primary NID, that code destroyed the
entire lnet_peer via lnet_peer_del_locked(), including the peer's
NIDs on other local nets that were still up. For a multi-rail
peer this discards a rail that is still reachable.

PtlRPC freezes a peer's primary NID in connection->c_peer.nid
and reuses it as the destination of every RPC on the import.
When LNet deletes the peer out from under it, that cached NID no
longer resolves to a multi-rail peer: LNet recreates a bare
single-rail traffic peer that knows only the now-unreachable
NID, and sends fail with -EHOSTUNREACH ("peer ... has no
available nets"). The client cannot fail over to the peer's
surviving rail, and the mount stalls until the deleted net is
restored, even though the peer was reachable the whole time.

Only destroy the peer when removing the net leaves it with no
locally reachable rail. lnet_peer_get_reachable_ni_locked()
looks for a peer_ni on a different live local net; if one
exists, the cleanup re-homes the primary NID onto that survivor
(unless LNET_PEER_LOCK_PRIMARY is set) and moves the removed-net
peer_ni back onto the remote peer_ni list via
lnet_peer_ni_clr_local_net_locked(), the inverse of
lnet_peer_net_added(). The peer stays in the table hash and
stays multi-rail, so the cached destination NID still resolves
and LNet selects the surviving rail; the demoted peer_ni is
re-bound automatically if the net is added back. Peers with no
surviving local rail are deleted as before, and the net == NULL
"remove all peers" path is unchanged.
