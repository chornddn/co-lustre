LU-20076 tests: shorten obd_timeout in recovery suites

A target waits 3 * obd_timeout for its clients in recovery,
and imperative recovery is not active in test sessions, so
with TIMEOUT=20 a test that drops a client before a failover
waits out at least 60 seconds. replay-vbr spends two thirds
of its time this way, and the other failover suites pay it
too.

Add set_recovery_timeout(), which sets obd_timeout to
RECOVERY_TIMEOUT, default 10, for the rest of a suite, and
call it from replay-single, replay-vbr, replay-ost-single,
recovery-small and insanity. The value is restored at suite
cleanup, or by the next suite's setup when a suite ends
without one, since the setting is persistent. TIMEOUT follows
it, so tests that scale with it still do. 0 turns this off,
and so does a HARD failure mode, where a node reboot takes
longer than the shorter window. The restored value is the one
the filesystem was formatted with, read from its config log.

replay-single test_200 delays a ping by three ping intervals and
races the pinger at 10 seconds, so it restores the full timeout
for its own run.

replay-dual stays at the full timeout. The reconnect
extensions of the recovery timer reach the 90 second hard
limit at 10 seconds, and when a lost client holds recovery
open until then, the clients still replaying are evicted:
test_21a and the tests after it fail.

Full suites on 3 nodes, with the previous patch: replay-vbr
1983 to 1338 seconds, replay-ost-single 691 to 437, insanity
1507 to 1043.

Across 30 days of master review sessions in Rainshadow, the
measured savings estimate a 0.7% reduction in average total
autotest session time per patch (6.5 million seconds saved out
of 918 million). Custom and subtest-change groups are excluded
from both counts. This is summed session time, not elapsed CI
time. replay-single and recovery-small are not counted in the
savings, since they were not timed.
