LU-20524 nodemap: do not orphan sub-ranges when deleting a range

The NID range of a dynamic sub-nodemap is stored in the interval tree
embedded in the range of its parent nodemap (lu_nid_range.rn_subtree),
and points back to that tree through rn_tree.

range_delete_generic() frees a range without looking at its subtree, so
deleting the parent nodemap's range - what 'lctl nodemap_del_range' on
the parent does in sanity-sec test_72a/72b - leaves every sub-range
linked into freed memory, with rn_tree dangling. The sub-ranges also
stop being found by range_search(), so their clients silently fall back
to the default nodemap. Deleting one of them afterwards, as the
recursive nodemap_del() of the sub-nodemaps does, then writes into the
freed parent range, which panics once that slab page has been returned
to the page allocator:

  BUG: unable to handle kernel paging request at ffff9ba0876d7460
  RIP: range_delete_generic+128 [ptlrpc]
   range_delete+0x25 [ptlrpc]
   nodemap_del+0x50b [ptlrpc]
   nodemap_del+0x1bb [ptlrpc]
   cfg_nodemap_cmd+0x2f3 [ptlrpc]

Move the sub-ranges up into the tree the deleted range itself belongs
to. They cannot conflict there: they were included in the deleted
range, which did not overlap any other range of that tree.

The subtree was introduced by e9278b8da1 ("LU-17431 nodemap: make
dynamic nodemaps hierarchical"), which already orphaned sub-ranges;
it became a use-after-free once range deletion started following
rn_tree.
