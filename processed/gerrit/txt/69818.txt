LU-7426 llog: order the updates of old-format update logs

In the old llog format, each update of an llog carries a copy of its
header, which a later update must not overwrite with an older copy,
so the updates of a remote llog have to land in order. An indexed
llog does not need that: each record and its index entry go to bytes
of their own, and a record that never arrived is a gap its reader
fills.

Make the update logs that can be written in any order indexed, and
order the updates of the old-format ones, from before an upgrade, in
the llog layer itself.

- New plain update logs are indexed, under an old-format catalog too
  (LLOG_CTXT_FLAG_INDEX). The current plain log is a new one at each
  mount, so after an upgrade no record is added to an old-format
  plain log. An update of an old-format llog on another MDT, adding a
  plain log to an old catalog, cancelling in it, or in an old plain
  log as it is emptied, is a transaction of its own, and holds the
  llog's lgh_lock until dt_trans_stop() returns. That orders the
  updates only once dt_trans_stop() of an update to another MDT waits
  for it to be sent, which the next patch in the series does: before
  it, osp_trans_stop() only queues the update.
- After a failed update of an old-format llog on another MDT, the
  llog header is read back, as what landed of the update is not known
  (llog_refresh_header()). Until that succeeds, the updates of the
  llog fail with -ESTALE.
- A remote plain llog gets its header and index in the transaction
  that creates it, rather than in that of its first record, which the
  other records then need not land after. llog_cat_new_log() returns
  the error of that transaction, a remote one too once the next patch
  in the series is applied. An indexed llog past lgh_max_size is full,
  as an old-format one is, the limit taken past its header and index.
- tests: OBD_FAIL_LLOG_NO_INDEX makes new llogs in the old format.
  replay-single 138a: upgrade from update logs in the old format.
