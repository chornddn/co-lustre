LU-21016 llite: complete hybrid DIO writes before commit

Hybrid IO sends large buffered writes as direct IO.  A direct IO
write is committed by the OST before it is acknowledged, since the
client sends the application's own pages and has nothing to resend
if the OST fails first: ofd_commitrw_write() sets th_sync for any
niobuf without OBD_BRW_ASYNC.  On osd-zfs that is txg_wait_synced()
for every RPC, so a switched write runs at a small fraction of
buffered speed, and on ldiskfs it is a journal commit per RPC.

The application asked for a buffered write, so give the switched
write buffered semantics: acknowledge it before commit and replay it
after an OST failure.  Copy the data into kernel pages, as the
unaligned DIO path already does, and send them with OBD_BRW_ASYNC.
The OST then replies with an uncommitted transno, ptlrpc keeps the
request on the replay list with the bulk pinning those pages, as it
does for a cached write, and brw_commit() lets them go when the OST
reports the commit.  The pages come from the page allocator rather
than the DIO page pool, which recycles pages regardless of their
refcount, so the bulk's pin is honoured.  fsync() still sends
OST_SYNC, so durability is that of buffered IO.

Bound the pinned memory as dirty pages are bounded:
osc_unstable_wait() holds a switched write while the OSC's unstable
pages reach max_dirty_mb or the global dirty limit, and after the
osc_enter_cache() timeout sends the rest of the IO as synchronous
DIO writes instead.  Such writes carry SOFT_SYNC once the OSC is
halfway to that limit, so the OST commits early.  On the OST, a
replayed niobuf with OBD_BRW_ASYNC but no OBD_BRW_FROM_GRANT had its
space accounted when first written, so tgt_grant_check() grants it
like a FROM_GRANT one instead of charging the free space again.

Add sanity 119v, which checks that switched writes leave unstable
pages until fsync, and replay-ost-single 14, which writes across a
replay barrier and checks the data after OST failover.
