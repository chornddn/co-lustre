LU-15248 tests: cap racer overstripe count relative to OSTCOUNT

file_create.sh picks random overstripe count [0, RACER_LOV_MAX_STRIPECOUNT]
for every file, and defaulted RACER_LOV_MAX_STRIPECOUNT to filesystem-wide
LOV_MAX_STRIPE_COUNT (2000).  That bound does not scale with number of OSTs:
on a small config like racer@ldiskfs+DNE with 2 OSTs it makes about half of
all files hugely overstriped -- hundreds to ~2000 stripes, i.e. hundreds of
stripes per OST.

Every whole-file operation on such a file -- truncate, a time/owner setattr
(both must reach every OST object), or a write spanning stripes -- builds a
client cl_io lockset with one extent sub-lock per stripe.  lov enqueues them
sequentially and synchronously and each granted sub-lock stays pinned until
whole io finishes, so the operation runs for O(stripes) RPC round-trips.
Under racer's concurrency that sweep outlasts server's ~100s lock-callback
timeout, and either the operation's own held sub-locks or a concurrent op
holding one of them (e.g. a write blocked on the object's truncate semaphore
behind the slow setattr) is evicted -- a data-loss event that also repeats
and blows the test's time budget.  Crash-confirmed on frozen-at-eviction
vmcores: expiring PW extent locks were sub-locks hundreds deep into
600-1900-stripe truncate/setattr locksets.

Cap default to RACER_MAX_OVERSTRIPE_PER_OST (16) times OSTCOUNT, clamped
to LOV_MAX_STRIPE_COUNT.  This still exercises overstriping (many stripes
per OST) heavily without the pathological tail, and both knobs remain
overridable for large-OST-count testing.
