LU-20797 llite: keep the I/O scope stable during an operation

LL_IOC_SET_IO_SCOPE and LL_IOC_FLR_SET_MIRROR rewrite fd_io_scope
without any serialization, so another thread on the same fd can
change the scope in the middle of a read, write, lseek, truncate or
fallocate that has already looked at it, and one syscall can then
mix two scopes, e.g. across an I/O restart.  The O_DIRECT check for
a mirror scope is also only done when the scope is set, so after
fcntl() clears O_DIRECT, a short direct read, or a direct I/O that
ll_direct_IO() hands back to buffered I/O, the data of one mirror
goes through the page cache.  Async readahead also reads the scope
after the read that queued it has returned.  A designated parity
mirror ends below i_size, so a direct read that stops at
ci_parity_eof with ki_pos still below i_size can fall back into the
page cache.

Take a copy of the scope under a new spinlock when an operation
starts, in ll_io_scope_snapshot(), and initialize every cl_io of the
operation from it, also across restarts.  A scope set while an
operation is in progress applies from the next operation.

Make a non-FILE scope direct I/O only: read and write fail with
-EINVAL unless the kiocb is IOCB_DIRECT, mmap fails with -EINVAL,
ll_direct_IO() does not fall back to buffered I/O for a designated
mirror, and ll_read_folio() rejects a page cache miss for one as a
backstop.  Cap a parity-scope read at ci_parity_eof in
vvp_io_read_start() so it returns a short read or EOF instead of
falling into the page cache.  Async readahead always uses the whole
file.

Add sanity-flr test_213c for the DIO-only and stable-scope rules,
and sanity-ec test_5c for reading a parity mirror up to its EOF.
