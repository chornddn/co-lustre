LU-20908 lfs: add lfs rebalance to level OST free space

OSTs of a pool fill unevenly, and a refilled or new OST takes most of
the new writes until it catches up.  Evening them out by hand with
lfs find and lfs migrate rewrites whole files and leaves choosing them
to the administrator.

Add "lfs rebalance [--pool POOL] MOUNT", on the mover of lfs drain.
The target of each OST of the set is the use percentage of the whole
set, so that OSTs of different sizes end even, or with --threshold PCT
the free space at that use.  On each OSS, the
OSTs of the set there that are short of it by more than --tolerance
(5% of the target) are the sources.  At most --parallel of them at a
time are closed to new objects, read as LFU Object Streams, and a
sample of their objects is queued, by the file each names: a little
more than the shortfall, by a hash of the object FID so that it is
spread over the OST.  Each object is moved with
llapi_ost_object_migrate() to an OST the MDS chooses.  A statfs every
few seconds, counting what was moved and not destroyed yet, ends a
source once it is within the tolerance, and opens it again.  Passes
repeat, with a bigger sample for a source left short, until no source
is short.

A file that is busy is set aside and retried, but never moved under a
group lock, and skipped in the end.  Objects that name no file are
left, and without --inodes so are those that hold no data.  --inodes
levels free inodes, sampling objects smaller than the mean object
size.  --find takes the lfs find predicates an OST object answers,
--size, --uid, --gid and --projid, with the meaning lfs find gives
them.  The sample and --find choose files: all of a chosen file's
objects on the source are moved.
--dry-run shows each OST's target, role and use before and after from
statfs alone.  --bandwidth, --threads, --json and --stats are as for
lfs drain.  The OSTs a rebalance closed are noted under
/run/lfs-rebalance with its pid, and locked while it runs, so that a
run stopped hard has them opened by the next one and an OST another
live run on the OSS sheds stays closed and counts in the target.  A
drain that closes one removes the note, and the run that closed it
then leaves it closed; a source closed by anyone else during the run
is neither closed nor opened by it.

Add sanity-pfl tests 30m to 30o: levelling with --dry-run and a
second run that has nothing to do, --threshold with --find leaving
small objects, the usage errors, and an OST closed by a live or a
gone run.
