LU-20949 osc: do not lock page cache folio for encryption

Before encrypting a page cache folio for a write RPC,
osc_brw_prep_request() was calling folio_trylock() on it. When the
trylock failed, the folio was assumed to stay locked during
encryption, as when cl_page_assume() holds it while waiting for
writeback. But the folio could also be locked briefly by a thread that
does not wait for writeback: a page fault through mmap, reclaim, page
migration, or drop_caches. When that thread unlocked it before the
check in osc_encrypt_pagecache_blocks(), the WARN_ON_ONCE() fired and
the write failed with -EINVAL.

The folio lock is not needed: the folio is only read, to encrypt it
into a bounce folio, at the inode and file index given by the caller.
Its content is stable until the RPC completes, because it is under
writeback for an async write, and anything that modifies or moves it
waits for writeback, or because it is locked by the IO that owns it
for a sync write. So do not lock the folio, and remove the lock check
from both the embedded llcrypt and in-kernel fscrypt variants of
osc_encrypt_pagecache_blocks(), which no longer need the directio
argument. Instead, check in osc_brw_prep_request() that a page cache
folio is under writeback, or locked for a sync write, with only a
WARN_ON_ONCE() that does not fail the write: if the folio changed
during encryption, it would be written again, as without encryption.

Add sanity-sec test_66e which does async and sync buffered writes to
an encrypted file, the latter by truncating in the middle of a page,
while mmap readers fault in its pages. Without the fix, it fails with
the WARN_ON_ONCE() in osc_encrypt_pagecache_blocks() and an EIO from
fsync.
