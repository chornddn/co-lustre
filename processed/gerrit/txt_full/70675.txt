LU-21178 lov: use a separate lu_site per lovsub device

Every stripe object of every cached inode is hashed in one table,
lov_device::ld_site, because lovsub_device_init() hands the OSC device
below it the site of its LOV device.  On a client caching 500M stripes
that hash reaches 100.7M objects, the 75% growth threshold of a 2^27
bucket table, and rht_deferred_worker() then retries forever an
allocation of 2^28 buckets (0x80000040 bytes) that __kvmalloc_node()
refuses with a WARN as it exceeds INT_MAX.

Give each lovsub device its own site, set up in lovsub_device_init()
and released in lovsub_device_free(), and stop passing the LOV site to
cl_type_setup().  An OST object belongs to one OST index, hence to one
sub-device and one hash, so the coh_parent check in lov_init_sub() and
the wait queue used by lov_subobject_kill() do not change.

This divides the objects per hash by the number of OSTs, so a table
that large becomes far less likely; it does not remove the limit for a
filesystem with few or unevenly loaded OSTs.  The LOV device keeps its
site, which no longer holds objects; removing it is left for later.

Each site also carries a bucket array of max(256, 2 * CPUs) entries, a
per-CPU stats block and a percpu counter, and lu_objects_flush() and
the site shrinker walk every bucket of every site, so the split
multiplies that fixed cost by the number of targets and makes them
walk bucket arrays that sub-sites, whose objects are normally held by
their parents and not on the LRU, have nothing in.  At a few hundred
targets that is noise; at a few thousand it is measurable.  Smaller
bucket arrays for sub-sites, and skipping sites with nothing to
reclaim, are follow-up work to be chosen on measurement.
