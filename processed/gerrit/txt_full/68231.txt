LU-20624 utils: fix stale fd in cb_get_dirstripe

cb_get_dirstripe() closes the descriptor it was given and stores an
O_NOFOLLOW reopen through its argument when LL_IOC_LMV_GETSTRIPE
answers ENOTTY, which it does for the fake symlink a foreign file or
directory presents and for any directory that is not on Lustre at all.
cb_find_init() and cb_getstripe() pass the address of a local copy, so
llapi_semantic_traverse() keeps the number that was closed: it
fdopendir()s a closed descriptor and skips every object below that
directory in silence, closes the number a second time on the way out,
and leaks the one that was opened.  The second close is the dangerous
one, since by then the walk may have handed that number to another
thread.

lfs find loses the subtree under any predicate that needs the
directory stripe: --printf, --links, --mdt-count, --mdt-hash and
--foreign.  lfs getdirstripe and lfs getstripe -D reach the same call
through cb_getstripe(), but they stop at the ENOTTY in any case, so
what the descriptor costs them is the double close rather than a lost
walk.

Reproduced with a tmpfs mounted inside a Lustre mount point.  lfs find
--printf over it prints one error line where it should print four
paths.  strace of lfs getdirstripe -r shows the descriptor half, the
two runs identical until the last close: the number cb_get_dirstripe()
closed is handed straight back by the next open, and the close that
follows fails EBADF here, or lands on a descriptor another worker
holds once lfs find is given --threads.

lfs find also reports the wrong errno for it.  cb_get_dirstripe()
returns the ioctl's -1 rather than a negative errno and cb_find_init()
passes that out unchanged, so the walk fails with "Operation not
permitted" where the error was ENOTTY.  That is left alone here.

The third caller, in the dangling-symlink arm of cb_getstripe(), opens
a descriptor it closes itself, and is correct as it stands.
