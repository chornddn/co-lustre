LU-12571 llite: deterministic reproducers for the umount LBUG

This change makes the intermittent LU-12571 umount crash deterministic so
that the fix ("LU-12571 llite: drain straggling cl_objects before
cl_sb_fini") can be exercised, and adds sanity-sec test_54a and test_54b.

Two fail_locs model the two ways a straggling reference reaches
->put_super:

OBD_FAIL_LLITE_FSCRYPT_UMOUNT_PIN (0x143a) models a pinned *dentry*.  On a
subdir (fileset) mount, /.fscrypt is presented from the real fs root
(LU-15176), instantiating a cross-tree dcache subtree whose regular-file
inodes carry cl_objects.  ll_splice_alias() pins (dget) those dentries and
client_common_put_super() schedules a short delayed work that releases
them.  The reclaim then happens in the umounting thread, so cl_inode_fini()
has completed by the time the drain loop re-tests.

OBD_FAIL_LLITE_UMOUNT_IGRAB_PIN (0x143b) models a pinned *inode* held by a
foreign thread, which is what ll_lock_cancel_bits() does: it takes the
inode with igrab() via ll_inode_from_resource_lock() and iput()s it from
the ldlm callback thread.  ll_splice_alias() igrabs regular-file inodes and
the same delayed work iputs them, so evict() -- and therefore
cl_inode_fini() -- runs on the workqueue thread rather than on the
umounting one.  The same thread is additionally stalled for 500ms in two
places: in ll_delete_inode(), standing in for the OST RPCs
cl_sync_file_range() issues there, and in vvp_object_free(), standing in
for the work lu_object_free() does before lu_object_fini() hands the device
reference back.  Those two stalls make the two windows observable -- the
first is the one an sb->s_inodes-based drain cannot cover, the second the
one a site-object-hash-based drain cannot cover.

sanity-sec test_54a drives 0x143a end to end: it subdir-mounts an encrypted
vault, arms the fail_loc, unlocks via the virtually-presented /.fscrypt,
and unmounts.  test_54b drives 0x143b, which needs no encryption: three
regular files read on a fresh mount are enough.

The number of pins is capped, at three by default and at fail_val when that
is set.  Each one costs the release thread about a second of the umount's
finite drain budget, so an uncapped injection on a lookup-heavy workload
would exhaust even a fixed client and LBUG it -- the opposite of what the
reproducer is for.

These reproducers are intentionally invasive -- they add a pin path to the
dentry lookup and umount paths and a stall to inode eviction (all behind
fail_locs, no-ops in production) -- and are provided as a separate change
to demonstrate the fix; they need not land with it.
