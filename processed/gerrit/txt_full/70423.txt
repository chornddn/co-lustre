LU-20730 llapi: read and write LFU Object Stream chunks

A scan hands its consumer one struct llapi_scan_rec per callback.
The record holds pointers and file descriptors, so it cannot leave
the process, and a consumer that wants many objects at once has to
batch them itself. The LFU Object Stream fixes that format: a bulk of
fixed-size slots, each holding one Cap'n Proto LfuChunk message with
a list of LfuInode records and their EA values, defined in
lfu.capnp by Timothy Day.

Add lfu.capnp to include/uapi/linux/lustre, installed beside the
headers, and lustre_lfu_capnp.h with its enum values and the field
offsets the schema compiles to, so C needs no Cap'n Proto compiler.

Add liblustreapi_lfu.c, a codec for the part the Lustre producers
send: LfuChunk with an empty or inodes union, LfuInode, LfuEa and the
skipped list. The writer lays a chunk out in place, records from the
front and EA values from the back, and writes the list headers last,
so no batch is staged. It is internal (lustreapi_lfu.h) until a
producer uses it, and is built as liblfu_codec.la, linked into
liblustreapi and into the test. The reader is public:
llapi_lfu_chunk_parse(), llapi_lfu_chunk_inode() and
llapi_lfu_chunk_skipped(). It reads in place and trusts nothing: every
pointer is checked against the segment, far pointers and zero-sized
struct lists are refused, and a struct shorter than the schema reads
as zero past its end.

llapi_lfu_test, run by sanity 56En, checks a chunk written by libcapnp
(pycapnp 2.2.4), a round trip over 16 slots, a record too large for
its slot and a jumbo chunk, a chunk of skipped objects only, refused
input, and every one-bit corruption of a chunk's first KiB. The bulk
the round trip writes ("-w FILE") decodes with libcapnp too.
