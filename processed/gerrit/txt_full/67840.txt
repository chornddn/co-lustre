LU-20564 utils: probe md RAID geometry for OST mkfs/mount

mkfs.lustre now probes the md array under an OST target, through any
stacked dm devices, and seeds -E stride/stripe_width so mballoc can
align allocations to full stripes.  mke2fs can derive these from the
blkid topology, but dm-cache replaces optimal_io_size with its block
size when that does not divide the row.  LVM thin and VDO pools are
not probed through, as they remap each block.  Nested md arrays such
as raid0 over raid6, and volumes that span one, are left to mke2fs and
the blkid topology.  raid0, raid4/5/6, raid10 and raidkm (level 71)
are decoded, and user-supplied values are respected.  mount.lustre
warns when an OST on a striped array has no fs geometry, or geometry
that is not a multiple of the array row, unless it is mounted with
-o stripe=.  l_tunedisk skips this check.

mkfs.lustre also warns about two things that cannot be corrected once
the OST is formatted: a partition that does not start on a stripe
boundary, and, on parity arrays, a data row (k * chunk) that is not a
power of two.  The default bulk RPC sizes are powers of two, so a row
that is not one leaves a partial row at the tail of every RPC, which
the array services as a read-modify-write.  It also warns when the row
is larger than the default 4MiB RPC, which can be fixed later by
raising brw_size on the OSS, up to its 64MiB limit.  A larger row is
reported as unfixable.  mkfs.lustre.8 gains a RAID ALIGNMENT section
covering these, and how to correct them.

Tested on RHEL 9.7 against live loop-backed md arrays: raid0/4/5/6,
raid10 near/far/offset, a non-power-of-two row, user-supplied values,
dm-linear over one array and spanning two, an LVM PV on an md
partition, dm-cache, and misaligned partition starts.  Also tested
against live raidkm arrays, plain and declustered.  In every case the
computed row matches md's own queue/optimal_io_size.  LVM thin and
VDO volumes on md get no geometry and no warning, and the mount
check was run against matching and mismatched geometry.
