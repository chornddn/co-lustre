LU-19823 lod: spread failover groups over the RR array

Without failure domains, the round-robin array only keeps the targets
of each server apart.  The targets of the two servers of an HA pair
can still be next to each other in the array, so consecutive stripes
of a file are on servers that can fail together, or be unavailable
together while one of them fails over to the other.

When no target has a failure domain, ltd_rr_failover_order() groups
the targets by the lowest NID of their primary and failover
connections, which is the same for all the targets that can fail over
to each other, and orders the array by group as for failure domains.
The groups are ranked by NID, first by network and then by address,
and lu_rr_group_order() orders them by the reversed bits of their
rank, so groups with close NIDs, likely in the same network or rack,
are mostly far apart in the array.  Unlike failure domains, this only
orders the array, and does not limit where the stripes of a layout or
its mirrors may go.  The failover NIDs are read from the import at
each rebuild of the array, so add_conn and del_conn take effect at the
next rebuild.

Add a -F option to lod_rr_test to put the OSSs in HA pairs, and check
that consecutive OSTs in the array are on different pairs.  Without
the ordering, "lod_rr_test -f -o 4-64 -s 2-16 -F" finds OSTs of one HA
pair next to each other in 1852 of 30512 combinations, and in none
with it.
