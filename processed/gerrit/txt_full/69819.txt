LU-7426 osp: send update RPCs to an MDT in any order

The update RPCs from an MDT to another went one at a time, in the
order of their versions, only to keep its update logs on that MDT in
order. With the update logs ready for any order, see the previous
patch, OSP keeps no order between update RPCs: no version, no sending
list and no sending thread. What depends on order between an llog's
updates, creating it before writing to it and cancelling its records
before destroying it, is done in transactions of its own, which wait
for their updates.

- The update request of a top transaction goes to ptlrpcd, and the
  top transaction waits for it along with the others; any other is
  sent synchronously. That is as before for a transaction with no
  write or destroy. A write or destroy in a transaction of its own
  (llog create, cancel, destroy) used to go to the sending thread, and
  dt_trans_stop() returned without waiting; now it is sent
  synchronously and its error comes back to the caller, as the
  previous patch and the rollback in llog_osd_cancel() rely on.
  A failed request fails no other: osp_invalidate_request() only makes
  the requests started before an eviction fail.
- A failed write no longer makes the object stale, and the list of
  written objects to invalidate when their request fails goes. The
  writes are mostly the llogs', which read back what they need
  themselves. A stale indexed llog made other writers fail with
  -ESTALE, or reread it with their records in flight; its failed
  record is a gap.
- When the update of an llog's cancel fails, llog_osd_cancel() puts back
  the bits, the count and the deleted marks of the index of every
  record it cleared, the last one too, so a later cancel can try
  again. An indexed llog on another MDT is not read back, as records
  are being added to it, and what landed of the update is not known:
  a restart reads its index.
- tests: OBD_FAIL_OUT_UPDATE_DELAY holds a modification OUT RPC on
  the MDT handling it, and OBD_FAIL_INVALIDATE_STANDALONE fails the
  update request of a transaction of its own. replay-single 138b:
  update RPCs to an MDT go out together. 138c: a failed one fails no
  other. 138d: an MDT fails with update RPCs in flight. 138e:
  destroys and other update RPCs go to an MDT together. 138f: a
  failed update of an old-format update catalog.
