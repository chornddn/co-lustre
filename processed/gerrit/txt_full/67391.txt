LU-15248 tests: cap racer overstripe count relative to OSTCOUNT

file_create.sh picks a random overstripe count in [0, RACER_LOV_MAX_STRIPECOUNT]
for every file, and defaulted RACER_LOV_MAX_STRIPECOUNT to the filesystem-wide
LOV_MAX_STRIPE_COUNT (2000).  That bound does not scale with the number of OSTs:
on a small config such as racer@ldiskfs+DNE with 2 OSTs it makes about half of
all files hugely overstriped -- hundreds to ~2000 stripes, i.e. hundreds of
stripes per OST.

Every whole-file operation on such a file -- truncate, a time/owner setattr
(both must reach every OST object), or a write spanning stripes -- builds a
client cl_io lockset with one extent sub-lock per stripe.  lov enqueues them
sequentially and synchronously and each granted sub-lock stays pinned until the
whole io finishes, so the operation runs for O(stripes) RPC round-trips.  Under
racer's concurrency that sweep outlasts the server's ~100s lock-callback
timeout, and either the operation's own held sub-locks or a concurrent op
holding one of them (e.g. a write blocked on the object's truncate semaphore
behind the slow setattr) is evicted -- a data-loss event that also repeats and
blows the test's time budget.  Crash-confirmed on frozen-at-eviction vmcores:
expiring PW extent locks were sub-locks hundreds deep into 600-1900-stripe
truncate/setattr locksets.

Cap the default to RACER_MAX_OVERSTRIPE_PER_OST (16) times OSTCOUNT, clamped to
LOV_MAX_STRIPE_COUNT.  This still exercises overstriping heavily -- many stripes
per OST -- without the pathological tail, and both knobs remain overridable for
large-OST-count testing.
