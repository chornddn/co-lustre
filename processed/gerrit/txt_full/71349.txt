LU-18938 obdclass: bound the delayed object free backlog

Since objects are freed by one background worker for the whole
node, a mass unlink can destroy objects faster than that worker
frees them. Nothing slows the unlinks, so destroyed objects and
their inodes pile up in server memory. The wait also makes each
free slower: by the time the worker reaches an object its inode
table block has often left the cache, so a long backlog keeps
growing on its own. A mass rm on an MDS reached tens of millions
of unfreed objects and over 100 GB of slab.

Bound the backlog. While fewer than lu_delayed_free_max objects
per site wait for the worker (one per 8 MiB of RAM, from 4096 to
65536), any destroyed object joins them, so a burst of unlinks
does not wait for frees. Past that, only ldiskfs objects larger
than osd-ldiskfs.delayed_unlink_mb (1 GiB by default, the tunable
from LU-16032 restored), whose truncate is expensive, are queued,
up to lu_delayed_free_large_max (3/2 of lu_delayed_free_max). The
destroying thread frees any other object itself, so a sustained
unlink load slows to the rate frees complete. osd-zfs marks no
object large: it already destroys large objects in the background
through its unlinked set.

The worker runs 100 ms after objects are queued, so that it frees
them in batches, rather than every second, and the shrinker no
longer frees the queued objects. A purge that cannot block, such
as the osd-zfs ARC prune callback or a lookup that finds the cache
over lu_cache_nr, still leaves the cached file objects it drops to
the worker, now within lu_delayed_free_max; the shrinker frees what
it purges itself. site_stats gains the number of objects waiting,
the number deferred and the number freed inline because a limit
was reached.

Measured on a rocky9 VM with 8 CPUs and one ldiskfs MDT, 8
threads unlinking 400k empty files, runs interleaved: 56.2 and
58.0 s with this patch (mean unlink RPC wait 0.94 and 0.97 ms, at
most 650 objects waiting), 54.0 and 54.3 s with
lu_delayed_free_max=0, which frees every small object inline
(0.90 and 0.91 ms). In earlier, slower runs the parent took 58.7
to 62.6 s and inline frees 58.2 to 60.3 s. The VM's disks are cached
by its host, so frees here are cheap and the backlog growth seen
in production cannot be reproduced.
