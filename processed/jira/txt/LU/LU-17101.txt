+1 on review testing of https://review.whamcloud.com/70346 (review-dne-part-2, RHEL 9.7): https://testing.whamcloud.com/test_sessions/59a403ed-f0f1-4b8c-8f58-c205fdf51470

The per-test debug logs show how the route goes down. The router marks both of its nets down and pushes that status to the peers. The two peers drop the route within 4 microseconds of each other:

272: 1794224544.063887 lnet_set_route_aliveness() route to tcp through 10.240.28.124@tcp has gone from up to down
270: 1794224544.063891 lnet_set_route_aliveness() route to tcp1 through 10.240.28.124@tcp has gone from up to down

Earlier in the same run, the push from the router at "Message forwarding enabled" gave the same pattern. Both peers marked the route up 1.3 ms after the router enabled forwarding. Separate ping timeouts on the two peers would not agree to 4 microseconds.

A router marks a net down in lnet_update_ni_status_locked(). The router does so when net_last_alive is older than router_ping_timeout + alive_router_check_interval. The test sets both to 5, so the limit is 10 s. lnet_parse() updates net_last_alive only for an incoming ping, a GET on the reserved portal. LST traffic does not count. Thus no ping from either peer reached the router for 10 s while LST ran.

LST traffic through the router also stopped. The first lst stat at LST start + 0.3 s worked. The second, 15 s later, got no reply from the far peer ("Failed to stat on 1 nodes"). The route went down 11 s after that.

The logs do not show why the pings stopped. Two causes fit the data:

	The pings wait behind LST bulk on the peer tx queue to the gateway. The peer credits are 8.

	The router socklnd stops reading because no router buffer is free. test_220 sets small_router_buffers=8 and large_router_buffers=4. TCP back-pressure then blocks the pings.

The debug mask did not include net, and autotest does not save the router buffer or peer credit counts. To decide between the two causes, it would be useful to run test_220 in a loop with debug=+net. On the router, read lctl get_param routers buffers peers each second.
