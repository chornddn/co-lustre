--- Description ---
socklnd never sends from the caller's thread: every message, a client's request and a server's reply alike, is queued and handed to a socknal_sd scheduler thread, which does the sendmsg. When the connection is idle that handoff is pure latency. socklnd could send directly from the caller when nothing is queued on the connection and the socket has room, and fall back to the scheduler thread otherwise (socket full, a send already in progress, or a caller that must not sleep).

Measured on two VMs (LNet selftest over a fast virtio-net switch), a small request's round trip passes through about six thread handoffs; on the server: softirq to socknal_sd for the receive, to the selftest worker, to another socknal_sd for the reply. Each costs 5 to 15 us under load and 20 to 40 us when traffic is sparse, against a server turnaround of 53 to 250 us. Sending inline removes the send handoff on both sides, two of the six.

Expected effect: roughly 20 to 30% lower latency for small RPCs and more RPCs per second at low queue depth, which is what metadata-heavy workloads (mdtest, small files) feel. Little effect on bulk. This is an estimate, not measured. o2iblnd already posts sends from the caller.

Size: medium. The caller must never block on the socket, ordering with already-queued messages must be kept, and the ZC request bookkeeping done in ksocknal_process_transmit() needs the same handling on the inline path.
