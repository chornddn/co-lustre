--- Description ---
When the clock on a server steps forward, every target on that server

evicts every export it has not heard from since the step. The clock can

step when a VM resumes from suspend, when chrony corrects a bad RTC, or

after "date -s". The console shows:

lustre-MDT0000: haven't heard from client ... in 318 seconds. I think it's dead, and I am evicting it.

Both clients and the MDT OSP exports on an OST are evicted, within about

one second of each other.

Cause

The ping evictor keeps export times on the wall clock.

exp_deadline, exp_last_request_time and obd_eviction_timer

are set from ktime_get_real_seconds() in __class_new_export(),

rev_import_init() and ptlrpc_update_export_timer().

ping_evictor_main() compares them with the same clock. A step of S

seconds makes every export look S seconds idle at the same time. When S

is larger than the deadline margin (about 100s with default timeouts),

all of these exports are evicted together.

Fix

Use ktime_get_seconds() for these values. They are only compared

with each other or printed as a difference, so they do not need wall

clock time. rq_deadline/rq_arrival_time (adaptive timeouts,

early replies) and scp_last_request also use wall clock time, but

they do not cause evictions and are not changed.

Reproducer

Mount a filesystem with an OSS on its own node and write from a client

to OST0000. On the OSS, stop chronyd/ntpd and step the clock forward

300 seconds:

date -s "+300 seconds"

Within a few seconds the MDT OSP exports and the client are evicted from

the OST. The new test replay-single test_225 does this and checks that

no target logs "haven't heard from client" and no client is evicted.

It fails on unpatched master.
