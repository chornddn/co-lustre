--- Description ---
socklnd learns that TCP has finished with the pages of a zero-copy send through its own protocol: the sender asks for a ZC-ACK, and the receiver sends a 24-byte NOOP back after it has received the whole payload. That is an extra message per zero-copy send, with an allocation, a scheduler wakeup, a sendmsg and a recvmsg on each side, and the sender's completion waits for the peer to read the payload and answer rather than for TCP to release the pages.

The kernel can tell a kernel socket user this directly. A caller can pass its own ubuf_info in msghdr.msg_ubuf with MSG_ZEROCOPY (upstream 6.0), with its own completion callback through ubuf_info_ops (6.10), and needs neither SO_ZEROCOPY nor the error queue. RHEL 9 and RHEL 10 both have these (backported to RHEL 9). Only io_uring uses it today. socklnd could embed a ubuf_info in ksock_tx, complete the tx from the callback (deferred to a scheduler thread, since it can run in softirq), and stop asking for ZC-ACKs. Nothing changes on the wire: a receiver sends a ZC-ACK only when the message asks for one, so old and new peers interoperate.

Expected effect: after LU-21217 a 1 MB transfer is about 18 packets, so the extra message is about 5% more packets and a few percent of CPU. Transfers just above zc_min_payload (16 KB) are 1 to 4 packets, where the extra message nearly doubles the message count, so perhaps 10 to 20% there. Send completions also arrive about one round trip sooner. Estimates, not measured. On RHEL 9 this path also gives real zero copy without relying on ->sendpage.

Size: a few hundred lines with configure checks (the reference rules for a caller's ubuf_info differ between upstream and RHEL 9).
